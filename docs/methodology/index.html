What is counted

The atlas covers ten nations and six leagues with one definition of nationality; three leagues and one player database are left out on purpose.

The ladder

Rung 1
NHL.
Rung 2
SHL (Sweden), Liiga (Finland), National League (Switzerland), DEL (Germany). "NHL + rung 2" is the headline's five leagues.
Home
The Czech Extraliga. For Finland, Sweden, Switzerland and Germany the home league is already rung 2.
Other
Everything else in the data.

Nations: Czechia against Finland, Sweden, Switzerland, Slovakia, Germany, Latvia, Denmark, Norway, Austria. Population: Eurostat, 1 January of the season's second year.

Nationality, one definition

A player's nationality is taken, in this order, from:

  1. citizenship where a league publishes it: the NHL's nationalityCode, the Liiga's and the SHL's nationality (the SHL's is mostly "N/A" before about 2010);
  2. the national-team roster at a World Championship or Olympics since 2010 (IIHF eligibility);
  3. eligibility or licence where that is all a source has: the Extraliga's foreigner flag, the DEL's licence nationality (from 2022/23), the National League's Swiss licence.

Players are linked across leagues on the folded name plus the birth date, so a Czech in the SHL is recognised from his NHL or Extraliga record. Czechoslovak-era births take the present-day state the NHL recodes them to. Of the 867 players on the pool page, 649 are Czech on eligibility alone.

Left out

  • KHL, by design. What this leaves out is described under Limitations.
  • AHL: its terms forbid automated access. AHL seasons appear only in NHL players' career records, a survivor-biased view.
  • Slovak Extraliga: its robots.txt blocks automated access. Slovakia is measured through its players in the covered leagues.
  • Elite Prospects: its terms forbid scraping.

Linking players across leagues

17 982 people in all, 14 303 of them with a nationality. Records are matched in four steps: folded name plus full birth date; first initial, surname and birth date (for Dan and Daniel); for records without a birth date, the name alone when exactly one person of that name played within four seasons and his birth year does not contradict it; and finally records still unmatched are joined within the same league on the same name.

Definitions

Games threshold
at least 20 of 82 games, pro-rated to the league-season's schedule (95th percentile of skater games played). The headline also reports any game and a flat 20.
Age
Season start year minus birth year. Question 3 uses 21 or under, question 4 uses 20 or under (under-21 hockey) and question 7 uses 23 or under for goalkeepers.
Population
Eurostat demo_gind, 1 January of the season's second year.
Complete season
A rung 1 + 2 season counts as complete when every rung-2 league has at least 90 % of its qualifying players' nationality identified.
Intervals
Counts of the whole published record are administrative counts and carry no sampling interval. The break model (question 2) reports 90 % highest-density intervals of the posterior. Means over seasons (questions 4 and 7) and medians over players or goalkeepers (questions 5 and 7) carry 95 % percentile bootstrap intervals, resampling seasons, players or goalkeepers, 10 000 resamples, seed 20260929 (src/analysis/intervals.py).
Season
Regular season only, every league.

Reproduction

From a clean clone, with Python 3.13 and uv (the environment is pinned in uv.lock):

make install            # .venv from uv.lock
make restore-snapshot   # data/snapshot/ -> data/processed/
make verify-snapshot    # check the files against data/snapshot/SHA256SUMS
make analysis           # outputs/*.json (about six minutes; the break model uses seed 42)
make pages              # docs/ from outputs/ and templates/site/
make test               # the test suite

make analysis-fast rebuilds everything except the break model. Each output file records the SHA-256 of the snapshot files it was built from, and the tests fail when the snapshot changes without the outputs being rebuilt or when docs/ is stale. Every number in the text is read from outputs/ at render time.

Limitations

  • Descriptive only The atlas is exploratory and was not pre-registered. Nothing here identifies a cause, predicts a season, rates a player beyond his published statistics or advises on selection or policy.
  • KHL The KHL is excluded by design (see Left out). Across the 21 tournaments since 2010, 25 % of Czech national-team roster spots on average came from KHL clubs: 33 % over 2010–2022 and 0 % over 2023–2026. The pool counted here is therefore smaller than the pool the national team drew on, by the most in the years to 2022.
  • AHL Not fetched (its terms forbid automated access). An AHL season appears only when the player also has an NHL career record, which favours those who reached the NHL.
  • Birth dates The National League and the DEL publish none, so their players' ages come only from a link to another league. That removes Switzerland and Germany from the age medians in question 3 and leaves the NL and DEL youth shares in question 4 unmeasured.
  • Nationality For 649 of the 867 Czech players on the pool page, the only evidence is the Extraliga foreigner flag or a licence, which is eligibility, not citizenship. The NHL counts (questions 1, 2, 5 and 7) rest on the NHL's citizenship field; the rung-2 counts for the National League and the DEL, and the pool page, depend on eligibility. The DEL published no nationality before 2022/23, so the rung 1 + 2 series before 2022/23 is a lower bound.
  • Name-only links Records without a birth date are linked on the name alone, only when one candidate exists within four seasons; ambiguous and unmatched counts are in the linking table.
  • Population The denominator is total population, not registered players. It is chosen because it is published the same way for all ten nations every year; participation differs widely between them (Germany has 83 million inhabitants and a small hockey base), so a per-million rate measures output relative to a nation's size, not relative to its hockey base. A handful of players moves a small nation's rate a lot.
  • Age bands Age is the season's start year minus the birth year. Question 3 uses 21 or under (the youngest band of its four), question 4 uses 20 or under (under-21 hockey), and question 7 uses 23 or under for goalkeepers, who are few per team; the three cut-offs are stated where they are used and are not interchangeable.
  • One season Questions 1, 3 and 7 are cross-sections of 2025/26. Counts of a few players change from season to season; question 3 shows the Czech 21-or-under cells for 2020/21–2025/26 for that reason.

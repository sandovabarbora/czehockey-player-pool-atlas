Question 1 · Czech hockey atlas · 2025/26

NHL and rung-2 players per million inhabitants, ten nations, 2025/26

In 2025/26, 20 Czech players reached the games threshold in the NHL, 1.83 per million inhabitants, which ranks Czechia 4th of 10 nations and above the median of the nine peers (1.21); with the four rung-2 leagues added, 53 players, 4.86 per million, rank 5th.

Evidence

What was counted For each nation and season, the distinct players of that nationality who reached the games threshold in the NHL (or in any of the five leagues), divided by the population. The threshold is at least 20 of 82 games, pro-rated to the league-season's schedule (95th percentile of skater games played): 20 games in a full NHL season, fewer in the short 2012/13 and 2020/21 seasons and in shorter European schedules. The counts are the whole published record, not a sample, so they carry no sampling interval; the effective n is the ten nations, and a rank moves with a few players: Latvia is 3rd on 6 NHL players.

Denominator Total population (Eurostat) is published the same way for all ten nations and every season; registered players are not, so the rate measures output relative to a nation's size, not to its hockey base.

Coverage In 2025/26 every qualifying NHL, Liiga and DEL player has a nationality; the National League has 21 of 365 unidentified (foreign licences with no match), the SHL 4 of 365. Only 4 seasons of the rung 1 + 2 series (2022/23–2025/26) have every rung-2 league at least 90 % identified.

What this means

Relative to population, the number of Czech players in the NHL in 2025/26 puts Czechia 4th of 10, above the middle of the peers and well below Sweden and Finland, and the rank is the same under all three thresholds. These are counts of the whole record for one season; a single rank position can turn on one or two players. The rate describes how many players from each nation reached the NHL per inhabitant. It does not measure how many people play hockey in each nation, how well any nation develops players, or why the counts differ.

What this does not show

  • Why the rates differ: participation, league structure and migration are not measured here.
  • The denominator is total population, not registered players. More
  • Some nationalities rest on eligibility or licence, not citizenship. More
  • The KHL is excluded, although it supplied a quarter of Czech national-team roster spots. More
  • A single season is a noisy cross-section for counts of a few players. More

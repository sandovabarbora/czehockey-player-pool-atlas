Question 2 · Czech hockey atlas · 1995/96–2025/26

Timing of changes in the Czech NHL count, 1995/96–2025/26

The observed Czech NHL count rose to 58 players in 2001/02 and was 20 in 2025/26; the break model places a downward step most likely in 2009/10, with a posterior probability of only 0.09 for that season, a factor of ×0.83 (90 % HDI 0.56–1.14) and P(level falls) = 0.80, its probability spread over 2008/09–2023/24.

Evidence

The model A Poisson local-level model, the same model the football atlas uses for its series: the log of the expected count follows a random walk plus exactly 2 step changes, and the step seasons are marginalised over every allowed pair (at least three seasons from the ends and from each other). Two steps are imposed on every nation, so a nation always receives two steps whether or not its series has them; the direction of a step is read from its interval, not assumed. 2004/05, the lockout, stays in the time index with no likelihood. Priors: σ ~ HalfNormal(0.2), step ~ Normal(0, 1), initial level ~ Normal(log mean, 1); step seasons uniform over ordered pairs, >= 3 seasons from the ends and apart.

The Czech steps The downward step is ×0.83 (90 % HDI 0.56–1.14); the probability that it lowers the level is 0.80, and its interval includes 1. The earlier step is dated most likely to 1998/99 (×1.47, 90 % HDI 0.73–1.97, P(level rises) = 0.85). Of the 6 steps in the table, 1 has an interval that excludes 1.

Diagnostics 4 chains × 1000 draws after 1000 tuning, seed 42; largest R-hat 1.007, 1 divergent transition over the three fits. HDI: highest-density interval of the posterior.

What this means

The observed Czech NHL count fell from 58 in 2001/02 to 20 in 2025/26, and the model does not place that fall in one season: the most likely season for a downward step carries 9 % of the probability, and the step's interval includes no change. The data are consistent with a gradual decline. The model describes when the level changed; it does not say why, and the timing coincides with other changes in the leagues that it cannot separate.

What this does not show

  • Cause. The upward steps in all three nations around 1998/99–2000/01 coincide with the NHL's growth from 26 to 30 teams [7], and the Czech decline overlaps the founding of the KHL in 2008 [8], a league the atlas does not cover. Neither is tested here.
  • A forecast of the count.
  • Descriptive and exploratory: no cause, forecast or selection advice. More
  • The KHL is excluded, although it supplied a quarter of Czech national-team roster spots. More
  • The denominator is total population, not registered players. More
